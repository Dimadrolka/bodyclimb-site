Dead hang test: how long should you hang?

There is no right number of seconds. Hang as long as your grip and shoulders stay comfortable, write the time down, and compare it only with your own last test. Fitness sites quote targets such as 60 seconds, but the ones we checked cite no study, and we found no adult norms by age with a primary source, so Bodyclimb does not show any.

How to do the test

  1. Check that the bar is solid. Dry your hands, or use chalk. Damp hands slip early.
  2. Stand on a box or chair so you can reach the bar without jumping.
  3. Take a full grip, hands shoulder-width apart, thumbs wrapped around the bar.
  4. Step off gently and let your arms take your whole weight. Start your timer.
  5. Hang on straight arms. Keep your ribs down, legs together and still. Breathe slowly; do not hold your breath.
  6. Stop when your fingers start to open, your shoulders start to hurt, or your form falls apart. Step back onto the box. Do not drop.
  7. Write down the seconds and the date.

How to read your number

Your number is a starting line, not a grade. Retest under the same conditions (same bar, same time of day, rested) and compare with yourself. A few seconds more than last time is progress, and a bad day is just a bad day. Skip comparing with strangers: age, body weight, hand size and bar thickness all change the result.

For context only: the US Marine Corps pull-up training guide uses dead hangs of 15 to 45 seconds as a training range. It is a training range, not a norm for your age.

What the hang is for

A dead hang builds the grip every pull-up starts from, and it is the first honest check before you work toward a pull-up. If you cannot hang yet, start with your feet on a chair or the floor taking part of the weight, and give your hands a little more each session.

Common mistakes

  • Hanging from the fingertips. Wrap the whole hand around the bar, thumb underneath.
  • Swinging. Step off slowly, squeeze your legs together and keep your eyes on one point.
  • Jumping up to the bar. Use a box, so you start and finish on your feet.
  • Holding your breath. In through the nose, then a long breath out.
  • Testing with damp hands. Dry them first. When your fingers start to open, step onto the box.
  • Testing when tired. Test rested, and the same way each time.

When to stop

Pain means stop. End the hang if your hands go numb or tingle, the front of a shoulder hurts or pinches, or your grip is about to open. Skip hangs after a recent shoulder dislocation or surgery.

If it is too hard today

Do a few short hangs of 5 to 10 s, or keep your toes on the box to take some weight off. For a longer hang, split it into three shorter ones of about 20 s with a short rest.

Questions

How long should you be able to dead hang?

There is no verified norm. Hang as long as you stay comfortable and compare with your own last test.

Is there a dead hang norm by age?

We found no adult dead hang norms by age with a primary source, so Bodyclimb shows none. The 60-second targets we found on fitness sites cite no study.

How often can I test my dead hang?

Bodyclimb suggests about every four weeks, rested and under the same conditions. The test itself is free on any day you have not marked pain or felt unwell.

Should I hang with straight arms and relaxed shoulders?

For the test, yes: a dead hang is relaxed on straight arms. If your shoulders feel loose or unstable, keep them pulled down a little (an active hang).

When should I stop hanging?

Stop if your hands go numb or tingle, the front of a shoulder hurts, or your grip is about to open. Pain means stop.
