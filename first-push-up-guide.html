How to do your first push-up: from the wall to the floor

Start with your hands on a wall and work down in steps: wall, kitchen counter, table, chair seat, a low step or your knees, then the floor. On every step, do the target reps with the same straight body line, then move to a lower surface. The lower your hands, the more of your body weight they carry, so the first floor push-up is the top of a ladder, not a test for day one.

What counts as a push-up

Your body is one rigid line from head to heels. You lower until your chest is about a fist above the floor, with your elbows at 90° or more, then push the floor away and rise as one piece until your arms are fully straight. No sagging hips, no half reps. This is the standard Bodyclimb uses for the floor push-up.

The path, step by step

Every step below is the same push-up with your hands on a different height. The higher your hands, the less of your body weight they carry, so you move down the list as you get stronger. In one study, a regular push-up put about 64% of body weight on the hands and a bent-knee push-up about 49% (Ebben et al. 2011, as cited by Kikuchi & Nakazato 2017). Move on when you can do the target with every rep clean, not by the calendar. The targets are Bodyclimb's own coaching standards for moving on, not norms for your age.

Knee push-ups and the low incline are the same tier: pick whichever you can do cleanly. No trial we know of compares incline against knee push-ups as the main route, so the order of steps is coaching practice, not a measured result.

Set up before any step

  1. Push on the edge of the surface first. A counter, table or chair must not slide or tip. Push a chair against a wall.
  2. Place your hands a little wider than your shoulders, arms straight, shoulders over your hands.
  3. Walk your feet back until you make one line from head to heels. Tighten your stomach as if someone is about to poke it, and squeeze your bottom.
  4. Breathe in as you lower, breathe out as you push up.

Common mistakes

  • Hips sagging. Squeeze your bottom and tighten your stomach, so head, hips and heels stay in one line. Reset at the top of each rep.
  • Elbows flaring out to the sides. Bring them to about 45° from your body, so your arms make an arrow, not a letter T.
  • Half reps. Go to the same depth every rep: chest touches the edge, or fist height on the floor. Do fewer reps if you need to.
  • Hips rising first. Push the surface away with your whole body at once, so your chest and hips rise together.
  • Dropping instead of lowering. Count two slow beats on the way down, three on your first floor push-ups.
  • Hips pushed back on knee push-ups. Shift forward over your hands until knees, hips and shoulders line up.
  • Reps getting shorter at the end of a set. End the set when you can no longer reach the same depth. Do not trade depth for one more rep.

When to stop

Pain means stop. Stop the set if a wrist, an elbow or a shoulder hurts, or if the surface you push on moves. If your hips sag or your depth gets shorter, the set is over. For wrist pain, try fists or push-up handles; if the pain stays, stop. If kneeling hurts your knees, use the low step instead of the floor.

If a step is too hard today

Step your feet closer to the surface: the more upright you stand, the lighter the rep. Or put your hands on a higher surface. On your first floor push-ups, you can do only the way down: lower for 3 s, rest your knees on the floor and come back up from your knees. Do fewer reps and keep every one full.

Questions

What if I cannot do a single push-up?

Start with your hands on a wall. Wall push-ups, then a kitchen counter, a table, a chair seat and a low step are the same movement with less of your weight on your arms. Move down one step when you can do the target with clean form.

Are knee push-ups OK?

Yes. In Bodyclimb, knee push-ups are an equal alternative to the low incline push-up. Keep one line from knees to head, hips forward, and go until your chest is just above the floor.

How long does it take to do a first push-up?

It depends on where you start, so no honest answer is a number of days or weeks. Move on when a step feels steady and the target reps are clean.

Is there a norm for how many push-ups a beginner should do?

Some fitness tests publish push-up tables by age and sex, but they score a test, not a goal for a beginner. Bodyclimb shows none: the reps in the table are its coaching targets for moving to the next step.

How do I get my first floor push-up if the incline is easy but the floor is not?

Practice the way down: lower for 3 s from the floor position until your chest is about a fist above the floor, rest your knees on the floor and come back up from your knees. Keep your body in one line.

What if I feel pain?

Stop the set. Pain in an elbow or a shoulder means stop for today. For wrist pain, switch to fists or push-up handles; if the wrist still hurts, stop for today.
