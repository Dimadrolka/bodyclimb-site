How to do your first pull-up (from zero)

To do your first pull-up from zero, build it in stages: hang from the bar, learn to set your shoulders, build a row base, hold the top, then lower slowly from the top (a negative), then pull with help. Each stage is a smaller version of the pull-up you can already do today. Nobody can promise how long it takes; it depends on where you start.

What counts as a first pull-up

A strict pull-up starts from a dead hang with straight arms and legs still. You pull until your chin is clearly over the bar and lower under control until your arms are straight again. No kick, no swing, no half reps. A chin-up (palms toward you) counts the same and is usually easier, so it is a fair first rep.

Set up before any step

  1. Check that the bar and its mount are solid, every session. A doorway bar must hold you.
  2. Put a sturdy box or chair under the bar so you start and finish on your feet. Step on and off it instead of jumping up or dropping down (the jump-start step is the only small jump).
  3. Take a full grip, hands shoulder-width apart, thumbs wrapped around the bar.
  4. Warm up first. Pull-ups go before anything else, while you are fresh.

Common mistakes

  • Shrugging. Keep your neck long and your shoulders away from your ears when you hold or pull.
  • Swinging or kicking. Hang completely still, squeeze your legs together, then pull.
  • Dropping through the last third of a negative. Count the seconds and slow down most when your arms are almost straight.
  • Stretching the chin over the bar. Keep your eyes forward and bring your chest toward the bar. If the chin does not clear it, the answer is not yet.
  • Half reps. Start on straight arms and come back to straight arms.
  • Holding your breath. Breathe steadily, out as you pull, in as you lower.
  • Too much too soon. Slow lowering is new to your muscles; extra reps in the first weeks mean heavy soreness. Do what the plan says.

When to stop

Pain means stop. Stop the set if you feel sharp pain in an elbow, hand or shoulder, if your hands go numb or tingle, or if you get dizzy. Skip hangs after a recent shoulder dislocation or surgery. For the first pull-up itself: after two tries in a day, stop and leave 72 hours before the next.

If a step is too hard today

Take weight off with your feet, cut the time into short hangs of 5 to 10 s, use a thicker band, lower in 3 s, or do fewer reps. A smaller version done well beats a bigger one done badly.

Questions

Can I do a pull-up if I cannot hang for 10 seconds?

Yes, you start earlier. Begin with a feet-supported hang where a chair or the floor takes part of your weight, and give your hands a little more each session.

How long does it take to do a first pull-up?

It depends on where you start, so no honest answer is a number of days or weeks. Move on when a step feels steady.

Do I need a gym?

No. A solid doorway bar or a park bar is enough for this line. A box or chair helps you start and finish on your feet.

Are chin-ups OK for a first rep?

Yes. Turning your palms toward you is usually easier and it counts the same.

What if I feel pain?

Stop. Sharp pain in an elbow, hand or shoulder, numbness or tingling, or dizziness all mean stop for today.
